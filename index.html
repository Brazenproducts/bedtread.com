Bed Mat vs Bed Liner — What's the Difference?

Bed mat: Sits on the floor of the bed only. Protects paint from scratches and impacts. Easy to remove, easy to clean. Best for hauling cargo occasionally.
Drop-in liner: Covers the floor AND side walls. More complete protection. Harder to remove. Best for daily hauling.
Spray-in liner: Permanent. Best protection. Can't be removed. Best for trucks used heavily for work.

For most truck owners who use their bed occasionally — camping, hardware runs, moving stuff — a rubber mat is the right answer. It costs less, installs in seconds, and does the job.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do truck bed mats damage paint?

No — properly designed rubber mats protect paint, not damage it. The issue is trapped moisture underneath a mat that never dries. Solution: remove and rinse your mat a few times a year. Quality mats have drain holes or ribbed patterns that allow airflow underneath.

What thickness should a truck bed mat be?

At minimum, 3/8 inch (10mm) for basic cargo protection. For heavy work use — tools, equipment, rocks — look for 1/2 inch (12mm) or thicker. Thicker mats also do a better job deadening road noise from the bed.

Can I cut a universal mat to fit my truck?

Yes — most universal rubber mats are designed to be trimmed with a utility knife or heavy scissors. Mark the cut line with chalk or a marker first. Take your time — you can always trim more, you can't trim less.

Do bed mats fit under a tonneau cover?

Yes — bed mats are low-profile enough to fit under almost all roll-up and folding tonneau covers. Bed liners with raised walls can interfere with some covers. If you run a tonneau, a mat is the better choice over a drop-in liner.
